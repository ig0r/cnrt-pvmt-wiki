Repair & Recycling

Part of: Preservation & Rehabilitation is-rehabilitation-strategy-of
Repair & Recycling is a rehabilitation strategy grouped under Preservation & Rehabilitation.
asphalt-overlayfull-depth-patchingpavement-rehabilitationrecyclingrehabilitationrubblization
Also known as: Concrete Pavement Repair, Full-Depth Patching and Rubblization, PCC Recycling, Pavement Rehabilitation
Created 2026-07-28 · Updated 2026-08-11

Repair & Recycling functions as a specific rehabilitation strategy within the broader scope of Preservation & Rehabilitation, addressing structural distresses that exceed routine preventive maintenance [1]. It encompasses pavement strategies that address structural distress by either replacing deteriorated materials or repurposing existing infrastructure to enhance load-bearing capacity. Full-Depth Patching operates on the principle of complete material substitution, removing the full thickness of distressed concrete and base layers to install new concrete and crushed stone foundations. In contrast, Rubblization functions as a recycling technique that fractures existing portland cement concrete slabs into granular pieces, utilizing the resulting debris as a high-strength base layer for hot mix asphalt overlays without total removal [1]. This approach destroys slab action in deteriorated concrete pavements, thereby preventing reflective cracking from joints and cracks into new overlays [1], allowing for effective management of existing pavement conditions with studies indicating it serves as a valid strategy for achieving long-lasting performance when structural integrity issues are present [1].

Structural Design Principles

Structural design principles for pavement rehabilitation prioritize transforming distressed concrete into a stable granular base or replacing it entirely to ensure adequate load transfer and longevity. [1], [2] In rubblization, the destruction of slab action necessitates analysis via Burmister multi-layer theory rather than rigid-pavement models, utilizing the resulting interlocked aggregate as a high-strength foundation for asphalt overlays. [1] Conversely, full-depth patching maintains structural continuity by matching existing slab thicknesses and employing smooth dowels to achieve high load transfer efficiency between new and existing concrete. [2]

Modified Rubblization Method proposed by Antigo [1]

Material Properties And Mixes

Material properties and mixes are critical determinants in pavement rehabilitation, influencing both structural performance and construction logistics across different techniques. In full-depth patching, the selection between rapid-setting accelerated mixes like M4 and conventional mixes such as C4 directly impacts heat of hydration and opening times, while increased patch depth enhances internal strength gain without compromising load transfer efficiency. [2] Conversely, rubblization transforms existing concrete into a high-strength granular base with specific modulus and layer coefficients that dictate structural design, requiring careful consideration of strain measurements and distress patterns to ensure the longevity of subsequent asphalt overlays. [1]

Effect of patch thickness on rebound numbers [2]

Equipment And Methods

Concrete pavement rehabilitation employs distinct equipment and methodologies tailored to specific distress levels, ranging from targeted repairs like Full-Depth Patching to comprehensive structural overhauls such as Rubblization. While Full-Depth Patching utilizes precise tools like Smooth Dowels for load transfer and relies on mix designs including M4 or C4 to manage heat of hydration and opening times, Rubblization involves large-scale demolition using specialized machinery like the Resonant Pavement Breaker (RPB) or Multiple-Head Breaker (MHB). [1], [2] These methods differ fundamentally in scope, with patching focusing on localized replacement and rubblization converting existing slabs into a granular base for subsequent asphalt overlays. [1], [2]

Resonant Pavement Breaker (Resonant Machines, Inc.) [1]

Illinois DOT Subgrade Rubblizing Guide [1]

Full-depth patching is categorized under Repair & Recycling because it involves removing deteriorated concrete to the full structural depth of the slab rather than performing surface-level repairs [2]. This method addresses distressed areas by replacing unstable bases with concrete, thereby ensuring the patch matches the pavement’s default thickness or specific design requirements [2].

Rubblization is a recycling method categorized under Repair & Recycling that mechanically fractures existing concrete pavement into a granular base layer using heavy-duty rubbling machines [1]. This process breaks the rigid pavement slabs into pieces ranging from sand size to 15 inches, effectively destroying slab action and allowing the material to behave like a high-quality interlocked unbound aggregate course [1]. By converting the deteriorated concrete into this stable base, the technique mitigates reflective cracking in subsequent asphalt overlays and eliminates the need for extensive pre-overlay repairs [1].

Performance And Distress Analysis

Performance and distress analysis for concrete pavement rehabilitation relies on evaluating structural integrity through methods such as maturity testing and strain measurement to determine optimal opening times and load-distributing capabilities. [1], [2] While full-depth patching utilizes heat of hydration and Schmidt hammer rebound data to ensure high load transfer efficiency, rubblization assessments focus on the mechanical behavior of fractured slabs under thermal and traffic loads using mechanistic-empirical design principles. [1], [2]
