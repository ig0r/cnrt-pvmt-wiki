Deflection Testing

Part of: Condition Evaluation & Nondestructive Testing
data-analysisdeflectometernondestructive-testingpavement-evaluationstructural-evaluation
Also known as: Pavement Deflection Measurement
Created 2026-07-28 06:45:51+00:00 · Updated 2026-07-30 00:04:50+00:00

Deflection Testing comprises nondestructive methodologies that evaluate pavement structural integrity by applying controlled loads and measuring the resulting surface deformation to assess stiffness and condition. Continuous Deflection Testing employs moving-load strategies, where the High Speed Rigid Pavement Analyzer uses time-coincident sensors for joint efficiency in concrete, the Rolling Dynamic Deflectometer detects subsurface defects via rolling accelerometers under sinusoidal forces, and the Rolling Weight Deflectometer calculates deflection by comparing spatially coincident laser profiles at highway speeds. [1]

Continuous Deflection Testing · Falling Weight Deflectometer

Pavement Deflection Measurement

Pavement deflection measurement primarily utilizes the Falling Weight Deflectometer to apply dynamic impulse loads and capture the resulting deflection bowl, enabling the backcalculation of structural layer moduli and the assessment of load transfer efficiency across joints. [2]–[4] While this method provides critical data for overlay design and distress identification, it is a stop-and-go process that contrasts with continuous testing approaches designed for higher-speed, system-wide data collection. [5], [6]

Example of composite pavement deflected shape (9-kip load) (cross-section view—exaggerated scale) [4]

Nondestructive Evaluation Techniques

Nondestructive evaluation techniques for deflection testing primarily utilize impulse load devices, such as the Falling Weight Deflectometer, to measure pavement structural response and back-calculate layer moduli without causing damage. [2], [4] These methods involve applying dynamic loads and recording deflection basins with multiple sensors to assess structural integrity, evaluate load transfer efficiency at joints, and verify overlay design performance. [2], [3], [7] While traditional stop-and-go FWD testing provides detailed data for mechanistic analysis and artificial neural network modeling, emerging rolling wheel deflectometers offer continuous, high-speed alternatives to overcome operational limitations on active roadways. [5], [6], [8]

Structural Capacity Assessment

Structural capacity assessment leverages Falling Weight Deflectometer data to back-calculate layer moduli and evaluate load transfer efficiency, thereby quantifying the pavement’s ability to withstand traffic loads. [3], [4] By analyzing deflection basins and isolating responses from specific components like dowel bars, engineers can verify overlay designs and detect critical distresses such as subgrade voids or joint degradation. [7], [9]

Example of composite pavement deflected shape (9-kip load) (plan view—exaggerated scale) [4]

Equipment And Sensor Systems

Deflection testing equipment primarily utilizes the Falling Weight Deflectometer (FWD) to apply dynamic impulse loads and capture pavement response via arrays of displacement sensors, such as geophones or velocity transducers. [2], [8] These systems generate deflection basins that are analyzed using backcalculation methods, including finite element modeling and artificial neural networks, to derive structural properties like layer moduli and load transfer efficiency. [1], [4], [8] While the FWD remains a standard for detailed structural assessment, alternative approaches like continuous testing aim to overcome operational limitations by enabling high-speed data collection. [5], [6]

Schematic of FWD deflection sensors [4]

Data Analysis And Modeling

Data analysis and modeling in deflection testing primarily utilize the Falling Weight Deflectometer to capture dynamic pavement responses, which are then processed through backcalculation techniques or Artificial Neural Networks to determine structural layer properties. [2], [8] These analytical methods normalize deflection basin data to standard load and temperature conditions, enabling the computation of critical metrics such as Load Transfer Efficiency and subgrade modulus while accounting for complex factors like curling and warping. [5], [9]–[11]

Schematic of the deflection basin under an FWD load plate [11]
