RapidAir Test

Part of: Air-Void Analysis is-automated-analysis-method-of
RapidAir Test is an automated air void analysis method of Air-Void Analysis.
air-voidsfreeze-thawpervious-concretetesting
Also known as: RapidAir, automated air void analysis
Created 2026-05-20 · Updated 2026-08-11

RapidAir Test is an automated air void analysis method for hardened concrete that utilizes a microscope camera to capture micrographs of concrete samples [2]. This approach employs automated image analysis to process these images, thereby replacing the manual point-counting techniques required by traditional linear-traverse test methods [2], and measures entrained air void structure — including spacing factor, specific surface, and air content — from polished cross-section images. It was used extensively in the integrated PCPC study to characterize the air void system of pervious concrete mixtures.

Overview of RapidAir Testing

The RapidAir test results for hardened concrete specimens can be correlated with fresh concrete measurements from the Super Air Meter, where SAM values above 0.2 are associated with spacing factors exceeding 0.20 mm. [1]

Testing Standards and Methodology

The RapidAir scanning test method employs a linear traverse method to measure air void parameters in accordance with ASTM C457, providing quantitative data on spacing factor and specific surface that correlate directly with freeze-thaw durability outcomes [1]. While the RapidAir scanning test method utilizes only five traverses per sample, alternative automated methods like the flatbed scanner may perform up to 150 traverses on the same specimen; this difference in traverse count contributes to variations in measured air void frequency and specific surface values between the two techniques [2].

Specific surface area (c) Spacing factor Figure 9. Comparisons of air void parameters obtained from flatbed scanner and RapidAir tests [2]

Sample Preparation and Procedures

The RapidAir test requires sample preparation involving cutting, polishing, and a surface enhancement technique that yields a black matrix with white air voids for automated image analysis [3]. Conducted on 4-inch by 8-inch cylinders in accordance with ASTM C 457, the device reports entrained air up to a maximum size of 4 mm [4]. To determine measurement consistency, each sample is rotated 90º between trials during the testing process [5].

Images of typical pervious concrete samples [5]

Measurement Capabilities and Limitations

The RapidAir device reports entrained air up to 4 mm in size, meaning the total air as measured by the RapidAir includes everything 4 mm and smaller (total air <4 mm) [5]. Unlike fresh concrete analyzers that measure voids ≤ 2 mm, RapidAir measures all void sizes observed under an optical microscope and considers the entire range of air void dimensions present in the hardened matrix [6].

Cumulative air content by void size for mixtures, measured by RapidAir [6]

In pervious concrete analysis, the RapidAir microscope camera is generally unable to capture voids larger than 3 mm, whereas flatbed scanners can detect these larger interconnected pores [2]. Consequently, for materials with large voids such as pervious concrete, the flatbed scanner method is often preferred over RapidAir [2].

Air Void Relationships and Characteristics

RapidAir test results for hardened concrete samples are often compared with permeable porosity measurements obtained via the boiling-water saturation technique described in ASTM C642 [4]. Empirical findings indicate a weak inverse relationship between total air content and spacing factor, where the spacing factor generally increases as the total air content of the concrete mixture decreases [3]. Specifically, empirical data indicates that concrete mixtures without air-entraining agents exhibit spacing factors ranging from 0.29 to 0.83 mm⁻¹, whereas mixtures with air entrainment maintain a maximum spacing factor of 0.22 mm⁻¹ [6].

Relationship between durability factor and % air and spacing factor of air entrained samples, obtained by RapidAir [6]

In pervious concrete, the delineation between entrained air and interconnected porosity is clearly observed at a void size threshold of less than 1 mm versus those greater than 1 mm [5]. A majority of entrained air in pervious concrete is sized between 0.01 and 0.1 mm, with natural air-entraining agents producing higher amounts of smaller voids (0.02 to 0.05 mm) compared to synthetic agents which produce more medium-sized voids (0.05 to 0.10 mm) [5]. Consequently, pervious concrete pore structures typically exhibit two distinct groups of air voids: a numerous group sized between 0.01 mm and 0.03 mm located in the cement paste or mortar, which are associated with pollution removal mechanisms, and a larger group ranging from 0.5 mm to 3.0 mm situated among aggregate particles that significantly contribute to water permeability [2].

Air void distribution in the pea gravel mixtures [5]

Performance Standards for Concrete Durability

According to ASTM C 457 standards referenced in testing protocols, acceptable ranges for hardened concrete air void parameters include a specific surface of 24–43 mm⁻¹ (600–1100 in⁻¹) and a spacing factor of 0.1–0.2 mm (0.004–0.008 in.) [3]. A spacing factor of ≤ 0.20 mm measured using microscopical methods is an indication of good freeze-thaw resistance, and this criterion was used to evaluate the acceptability of air void structures in field mixtures [4].

Empirical data shows that mixtures with high air content but inadequate void structure parameters—such as low specific surface and high spacing factor—may still exhibit poor freeze-thaw resistance despite meeting basic air content thresholds [1].

F-T durability results [1]

Application to Pervious Concrete (PCPC)

The RapidAir test was validated for pervious concrete in the 2011 integrated study, where it effectively determined entrained air void structure in PCPC by distinguishing it from the interconnected porosity. Even without an air entraining agent, PCPC exhibited spacing factor values <0.2 mm (200 µm), which is well below the critical threshold of 0.3 mm; this finding implied that improved density, rather than spacing factor reduction, is the mechanism by which air entrainment enhances freeze-thaw durability in pervious concrete [5]. This observation follows prior work that established a RapidAir spacing factor criterion of ≤ 0.22 mm for air-entrained concrete durability [6].

Furthermore, the study noted that in limestone aggregate mixtures, the total air content measured by RapidAir (<4 mm) increased with air entrainment, whereas in rounded pea gravel mixtures, porosity decreased due to improved workability allowing for more compaction [5].

Total air and entrained air for limestone aggregate [5]
