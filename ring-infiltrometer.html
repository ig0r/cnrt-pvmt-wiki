Ring Infiltrometer

Part of: Pavement Foundation & Support is-evaluation-method-for
Ring Infiltrometer is the field testing method used to evaluate drainage quality of Pavement Foundation & Support.
conceptdrainagepavement-foundationtesting
Also known as: infiltration test, infiltrometer
Created 2026-07-22 · Updated 2026-08-11

A Ring Infiltrometer is a field testing device used to measure the infiltration rate of pavement foundation materials, providing an indication of drainage quality within the Pavement Foundation & Support system. The test involves placing a ring on the pavement surface, filling it with water, and measuring the rate at which water infiltrates into the pavement structure. On the Michigan I-96 reconstruction project, ring infiltrometer testing was used to evaluate drainage quality across four test sections. While other field testing methods such as falling weight deflectometers, light weight deflectometers, cone penetrometers, nuclear gauges, rapid gas permeameter tests, and static plate load tests are also employed for pavement evaluation [1], the ring infiltrometer specifically addresses hydraulic performance.

Test Procedure Overview

  • Metal ring placed on pavement surface
  • Ring filled with known volume of water

Infiltration Rate Calculation Method

  • Infiltration rate calculated as volume per unit area per unit time

Sampling and Measurement Protocol

  • Time measured for water to infiltrate
  • Minimum of 3 readings at each of 8 locations per test section

Field Results Summary

| Section | Infiltration Rate (m/s) | vs. Threshold (1.00E-06 m/s) | |—|—|—| | TS1 | 2.16E-05 | 21.6× | | TS2 | 1.65E-07 | 0.17× | | TS3 | 8.50E-06 | 8.5× | | TS4 | 4.20E-06 | 4.2× |

Comparison to Drainage Thresholds

  • All rates exceeded the 1.00E-06 m/s threshold for “excellent” drainage quality

Discharge Coefficient Analysis

  • Field-measured Cd = 0.34 (“excellent”) vs. design assumption Cd = 0.38 (“good”) [1]
