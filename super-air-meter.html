Super Air Meter

Part of: air-void-analysis
aashtoair-void-systemdurabilityfresh-concretetest-method
Also known as: AASHTO T 395, SAM
Created 2026-07-22 00:00:00+00:00 · Updated 2026-07-29 02:56:42+00:00

The Super Air Meter (SAM) is an innovative test method that measures the air-void system quality in fresh concrete, providing a more comprehensive assessment than traditional air content measurement alone. The SAM test has been standardized as AASHTO T 395, “Standard Method of Test for Characterization of the Air-Void System of Freshly Mixed Concrete by the Sequential Pressure Method.”

Introduction to SAM Testing

Unlike the traditional Type B pressure meter that measures only total air content, SAM uses sequential pressurization to infer the quality of the air-void system [1]. The test applies three sequential pressures (0.21 MPa and 0.31 MPa) twice, and the difference in equilibrium pressure at the highest pressure between the two sequences is reported as the SAM number [1].

Sequential pressures applied in the SAM and calculation of the SAM number [1]

Test Principle and Correlations

The SAM number has been correlated to the air-void spacing factor obtained through ASTM C457 and the durability factor (DF) of concrete as assessed using AASHTO T 161. Furthermore, SAM results have been shown to correlate with hardened concrete properties measured by ASTM C457, where higher SAM values correspond to lower specific surface and higher spacing factors indicative of marginal freeze-thaw durability [2].

Apparatus and Limitations

The SAM test apparatus utilizes a modified pressure meter setup in which the traditional dial gauge is replaced with a digital gauge to facilitate sequential pressurization at 0.21 MPa (30 lb/in.²) and 0.31 MPa (45 lb/in.²) [1]. However, this test method is not suitable for lightweight or highly porous aggregates because the applied pressure compresses air within the aggregate, which interferes with accurate total air content measurement [1].

Schematic of the four-point Wenner array probe setup [1]

Specification Limits and Design Alternatives

AASHTO R 101 provides two design alternatives for air-void assessment: a mixture design requiring a SAM number ≤0.20 and air content >4%, and a construction acceptance requirement of a SAM number ≤0.30, which accounts for variability in air void systems, construction practices, and test method. The lower SAM number for mixture design creates a safety factor against failure in construction [1]. Furthermore, a SAM value below 0.2 is recommended to ensure an air-void spacing factor below 0.008 inches, which correlates with adequate freeze-thaw resistance [2].

Air content scatter data for bridges (left) and pavements (right) [1]

Field Performance Data and Comparisons

Based on PEM shadow testing conducted across 19 states, the average SAM number was 0.21 across all projects, with 14% of results exceeding the 0.30 acceptance limit [3]. Within these tests, NYSDOT achieved an average SAM number of 0.18 (5% above 0.30 limit) [3]. The difference between the Type B air meter and SAM averaged 0.1% with a standard deviation of 0.58% [3]. In performance-engineered concrete paving mixtures, fresh and hardened air contents of 7.5% and 6%, respectively, were recorded alongside a SAM number of 0.22 [3].

The expected standard deviation between SAM-determined air content and Type B meter results is 0.29%, which allows for a variation of up to 0.58% within a 95% confidence interval [1].

Air content measured by Type B meter (Air_Content) and SAM (SAM_Air) for various locations [1]

Training Requirements and Calibration

The PEM project conducted extensive SAM training across 12 states, utilizing typical agendas that included a pretest knowledge assessment, SAM presentation and theory, testing with water for calibration, concrete testing and SAM calibration, and both written and hands-on performance tests. Throughout this process, training emphasized that proper equipment maintenance and adherence to standard methods are critical for accurate results [1].

Key Findings and Durability Implications

A SAM number exceeding 0.30 indicates that freeze-thaw durability is a concern, as such mixtures are likely to suffer damage if they become saturated and subsequently freeze [1]. This underscores the importance of SAM testing to assess air-void system quality during construction rather than focusing solely on total air content, particularly because LTPP SPS-2 core analysis revealed that many pavements with fresh concrete air content meeting specifications actually had marginal to poor air-void system characteristics in hardened concrete [1].
