Stringless Paving

Part of: Concrete Paving Construction is-stringless-paving-method-of
Stringless Paving is a stringless paving method of Concrete Paving Construction.
conceptconstructiongpsmachine-controlpaving
Also known as: 3D paving control, GPS machine control
Created 2026-05-20 · Updated 2026-08-11

Stringless Paving is a method of concrete paving construction that utilizes real-time GPS positioning to guide pavers without physical string lines [2]. This approach enables automated grade control for concrete placement by employing noncontact sensors to tie the paving surface to a 3D geographic information system database [2]. By eliminating the need for traditional stringline guidance, this technology aims to mitigate surface defects associated with conventional systems and improve overall construction efficiency [1].

Overview of Stringless Paving

The TR-600 study wiegand-2010-improving-concrete-overlay-construction identified two key requirements for stringless paving, noting that a pre-pour meeting greatly improved coordination. Additionally, the Poweshiek County V-18 project was the first stringless paving project in Iowa [1].

Poweshiek existing pavement [1]

Technical Requirements and Control Systems

  1. Electronic/hydraulic controls on the slipform paver, requiring a five-day training course for contractor staff.

Stringless paving workflows require specialized software capable of plotting 3D profiles from GPS data against design models, with Geopac 3D, AutoCAD, and MicroStation identified as viable tools for developing existing and final road surface profiles. [1]

Vertical and Horizontal Accuracy Standards

  1. Vertical control points at 250-foot intervals on alternating sides of the roadway (vs. 1,000-foot intervals for conventional control), to maintain line of sight between total stations and control points.

Laser-based sensors used in stringless paving systems have demonstrated a vertical deviation of 0.4 inches. [2]

A high-performance stringless guidance system may achieve a deviation of 0.1 inches vertically and 0.2 inches horizontally. [2]

Achieving target vertical control deviations remains difficult when paving through horizontal or vertical curves and superelevated sections. [2]

GPS and Surveying Implementation

Stringless paving operations require the integration of GPS or total station systems with control system experts to ensure minimum pavement thickness and smooth transitions toward planned geometry [3]. To establish adequate vertical control using GPS, additional control points must be set approximately one-quarter to one mile laterally from the project edges at longitudinal intervals of one to two miles [1]. Furthermore, these operations can utilize a GPS-equipped all-terrain vehicle (ATV) fitted with a laser profiler and rover unit to map existing surface edges, wheel paths, quarter points, and centerlines for establishing final grades and estimating concrete quantities [1].

GPS receiver and laser profiler located on the top of the John Deere Gator [1]

Coordinate control for laser scanning systems requires tracking total stations monitoring paver-mounted prisms, which is combined with tilt sensors and accelerometers to correct vibration-induced errors in scanned profiles [4].

Anticipated vision of laser scanning concrete thickness profiler [4]

Surface Model Construction and Data Accuracy

Surface model building required close communication between the contracting authority, contractor, and model builder, as model accuracy depended on high-quality “as-built” survey data and accurate existing surface mapping. When properly implemented, no paving days were lost due to model construction or application after the initial learning curve. However, the accuracy of stringless paving surface models is limited by environmental factors such as dense foliage or large towers blocking radio signals, satellite configuration constraints, and insufficient communication speed between data collection equipment and RTN base stations [1].

An ATV equipped with a GPS [1]

Operational Challenges and Smoothness Issues

Stringless paving eliminates common pavement smoothness issues associated with conventional stringline systems, specifically the chord effect, sag effect, and random survey effect [2]. However, the spacing between robotic total stations controlling a paver’s elevation and steering can cause regular fluctuations in International Roughness Index (IRI) values, with the side of the paver further from the total stations exhibiting greater roughness; consequently, reducing the distance between these total stations has been shown to improve IRI results [5].

Furthermore, vibration from dowel-bar inserters and short wavelength surface irregularities imparted by oscillating correcting beams can cause real-time smoothness measurements to significantly exceed hardened profile readings, as these short wavelengths are removed during auto-float and hand finishing [5]. Through power spectral density analysis, specific joint spacing (e.g., 15 feet) and concrete load spacing (e.g., 13 feet) have been identified as dominant wavelengths contributing to pavement roughness in hardened profiles [5].

PSD Analysis Showing Joint Spacing at 15’ and Concrete Loads at 13’ with Associated Harmonic Wavelengths Contributing to Pavement Roughness [5]

Thickness Measurement Technologies

To achieve specific smoothness targets, stringless paving is utilized, which requires pavers capable of measuring their own three-dimensional position and the approximate elevation of the base layer [3]. One method for monitoring this process involves laser scanning systems, which can determine pavement thickness at any point by comparing pre-pave base scans with post-pave surface scans; this provides continuous data coverage to calculate thickness variance and identify areas below tolerance [4].

Paving train from Cedar Valley Corp. on IA-60 project [4]

Alternatively, electromagnetic sensors offer a nondestructive method for real-time thickness measurement by detecting conductive plates placed on the subgrade, offering potential for handheld or paver-mounted implementation [4]. Similarly, eddy current testing can detect conductive materials like aluminum plates beneath wet concrete, enabling on-the-fly thickness monitoring without requiring dry conditions or post-pave coring [4].

Eddy current sensor for concrete thickness measurement [4]

Milling and Volume Control

Stringless milling guided by a computer model allows existing pavement to be milled precisely to the design profile and cross-slope, which reduces concrete quantity overruns compared to conventional methods [6]. For example, one project reduced actual concrete placement overrun from 126% on plans to 104% using a surface model based on nine-shot cross sections at 50-foot intervals [6].

– Milling to Grade Using Automatic Machine Control [6]

Furthermore, stringless milling enables the elimination of longitudinal joint patching between original pavement and existing widening units by milling the widening to a uniform depth, thereby reducing the number of longitudinal joints from two to one [6]. This process improves overlay depth uniformity and reduces the risk of concrete overruns [6].

– Pennsylvania SR-119 Partial Typical Section [6]

Traffic Management and Operational Efficiency

Stringless construction technologies decrease the width of roadway needed for construction equipment, which can eliminate the need for detour widening and allow for two-way construction traffic alongside through traffic [6]. This reduction in required workspace enables more efficient maintenance of traffic plans that accommodate standard clearances for automatic machine control [6].

Furthermore, stringless technology facilitates rapid relocation of paving spreads between multiple work zones without the limitations of stringline placement and removal, allowing contractors to operate two simultaneous paving areas to maintain continuous production schedules [6]. Equipment moves between sites in less than one day, supporting operations that run approximately six out of seven days a week [6].
