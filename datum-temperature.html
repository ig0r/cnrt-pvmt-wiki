Datum Temperature

Part of: Maturity Method
concretematuritytemperaturetesting
Also known as: T₀

The temperature below which concrete has no strength gain with time. In the Nurse-Saul equation, TTF = ∫(T - T₀)dt, where T₀ is the datum temperature. For OPC, the generally accepted value is -10°C, due to fine pores and high alkali concentration that depress the freezing point of pore solution. [1]

Effect of datum temperature on the strength-TTF-time correlation [1]

Overview of SCM Concrete Findings

  • All SCM concretes tested had datum temperatures higher than -10°C (ranging from -7.17°C to -0.81°C)

Blended cement research has shown that SCM concrete requires a higher Datum Temperature than the standard -10°C used for OPC, with a range of -7.17°C to -0.81°C, and Activation Energy also increases with SCM content [1].

Impact of Slag and Ternary Blends on Datum Temperature

  • Datum temperature increases linearly with slag replacement
  • Ternary cements (OPC+fly ash+slag) had higher datum temperatures than binary cements (OPC+fly ash)

Hydration Sensitivity and Activation Energy

Because SCM concrete stops hydrating at a higher (warmer) temperature than OPC concrete, it must be placed and cured at warmer temperatures. Furthermore, ternary cement concretes exhibit higher activation energies than binary cement concretes, indicating a greater sensitivity to temperature changes during hydration [1].

Type I, binary, and ternary cements, respectively. The initial set time decreased 0.9, 0.8, and 1.1 hours for the concrete made with Lafarge Type I/II, binary, and ternary cements, respectively. Note that the set time drops of binary/ternary cement concrete are comparable with or less than that of OPC concrete. This indicates that binary/ternary cement concrete will have equal or less slump loss to/than OPC concrete under a hot weather. It is of great benefit to use SCM concrete for hot weather concreting. (a) Holcim cement [1]

Implications for Maturity Estimation

  • Using the standard -10°C datum temperature for SCM concrete under-estimates maturity

Consistency Requirements in Strength-Time Correlations

If the same datum temperature is used for both strength-TTF correlation and TTF-time correlation, the pavement opening time decision remains unaffected. Consequently, the key requirement is consistency between the two correlations rather than the absolute value.

While using the wrong datum temperature does NOT affect pavement opening time IF the same value is used for both strength-TTF and TTF-time correlations, using TTF requirements from normal-temperature (70°F) curing for cold-weather (50°F) concrete can lead to premature traffic opening, where concrete is at ~3000 psi instead of 3500 psi; therefore, strength-TTF correlations must be developed at the expected field curing temperature, as maintaining a consistent datum temperature between correlations is the critical requirement [1].

Maturity Calculation via the IntelliROCK System

The IntelliROCK System calculates maturity using the Nurse-Saul method at a user-selected datum temperature, which was set to -10°C in field evaluations [2]. Depending on the software configuration of the monitoring device, maturity calculations can be performed in either Fahrenheit or Celsius degree-hours [2]. To achieve an accurate determination of pavement opening strength, a minimum of four temperature readings per day is required to capture maximum and minimum values [2].

IntelliROCK System (Nomadics Construction Laboratory) [2]

Implementation Guidelines for Accurate Pavement Opening

Using a datum temperature derived from normal weather conditions to determine opening times for concrete cured in cold weather can lead to premature pavement opening and potential structural failure. [1]
