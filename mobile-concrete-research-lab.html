Mobile Concrete Research Lab

Part of: MCO Suite of Tests is-physical-infrastructure-of
Mobile Concrete Research Lab is the physical infrastructure of MCO Suite of Tests.
field-equipmentquality-controltesting
Also known as: MCRL, Mobile Lab
Created 2026-05-20 · Updated 2026-08-11

The Mobile Concrete Research Lab is a 44-ft Featherlite gooseneck trailer equipped with a full suite of concrete testing apparatus, serving as the mobile physical infrastructure that enables the execution of MCO Suite of Tests in field environments rather than fixed laboratories. Designed and deployed by the MCO Project (TPF-5(066)) to bridge the gap between laboratory and field testing, its design specifications were developed based on the requirements of the MCO project suite of tests to ensure appropriate space for procedures [1]. Towable by a medium-duty truck, it allows researchers to conduct preliminary test development and data collection directly at participating sites, facilitating the evaluation of these tests outside traditional laboratory settings [1]. The lab has been deployed to 16 state shadow projects across the US [1].

Overview of Mobile Laboratory Role

Funded by ACPA and 14 state/regional paving associations, the mobile lab enabled consistent, high-quality testing across diverse state projects using local materials and mixes [1]. This approach ensured uniform data collection for the 16-state study [1] through connections to the MCO Suite, Air Void Analyzer, and Coffee Cup Test.

Inside the Mobile Lab [1]

Material Testing and Deployment Strategy

The mobile laboratory was deployed on-site to perform material testing and document the construction process during concrete overlay projects in Delaware, Pennsylvania, and South Dakota. [2]

– Placement of the 6” Bonded Concrete Overlay on Pennsylvania SR-119 [2]

Gradation and Mortar Analysis Equipment

Testing procedures include the use of sieves and sieve shakers for combined gradation, a flow table for mortar flow, and both the mortar penetrometer and Vicat apparatus to determine set time (ASTM C403). Field testing revealed that sieved mortar samples exhibited significantly less variation than full concrete specimens during simple isothermal calorimetry tests [3]. Consequently, using mortar samples extracted from field concrete was recommended over whole concrete samples to ensure consistency in field calorimetric measurements [3].

Isothermal calorimetry results for US 71 (Atlantic, IA) field concrete samples [3]

Air Void and Consistency Measurement Tools

The mobile lab was utilized to routinely test plastic and hardened concrete properties, specifically measuring air voids, slump, compressive strength, and flexural strength during daily paving operations [4]. Testing included the use of AVA with vibration-isolation base (air void system analysis) and pressure air meters (ASTM C231).

Thermal and Hydration Monitoring Systems

The mobile lab utilizes iQdrum calorimeters for heat signature monitoring, as well as maturity loggers through the Command Center and intelliRock systems. The lab’s semi-adiabatic calorimetry tests using the AdiaCal device demonstrated high sensitivity to concrete placement temperature, which resulted in variable thermal curves even within samples tested on the same day [3]. This variability made the AdiaCal results suitable for predicting field set times but unsuitable for precise quality control applications [3].

AdiaCal calorimeter [3]

Weather and Environmental Data Collection

To correlate atmospheric conditions with concrete setting rates, the mobile lab’s deployment facilitated the collection of daily weather data at both the beginning (BOP) and end (EOP) of paving operations [4], utilizing a weather station (Davis Vantage Pro).

Wind speed instrument [4]

Advanced Predictive Software and Modeling

The HIPERPAV software, which is used for saw-cut window and cracking prediction, achieved higher accuracy in its predictions when using hydration curve parameters derived from semi-adiabatic test data compared to those derived from isothermal tests [3]. This improved accuracy was attributed to the use of concrete specimens rather than cement mortar and the fact that the semi-adiabatic condition aligns more closely with in-situ pavement temperatures [3].

Predicted pavement temperatures versus measurements (pavement top, Alma Center, WI ) [3]

Microwave Water-to-Cement Ratio Testing

Because neither isothermal nor AdiaCal calorimeters could effectively identify changes in field concrete w/c ratios, the mobile lab’s microwave water-to-cement ratio testing method served as a necessary supplementary tool [3]. This method involved heating sieved mortar samples until weight differences between consecutive two-minute intervals fell below 0.1 g [3]. In South Dakota, microwave water content testing was performed on six samples to verify consistency across batches during overlay construction [2].
