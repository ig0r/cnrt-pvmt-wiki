Advanced finite element program for the analysis and design of rigid pavement systems, extensively tested and validated for over 20 years. Developed from ILLI-SLAB (Tabatabaie and Barenberg 1978; Khazanovich 1994; Khazanovich et al. 2000). [1]

Pavement geometry and finite element mesh used for the ILLI-PAVE runs [1]

Overview of ISLAB2000

The program structurally models JPCP and CRCP systems built as new pavements or used as overlays within the MEPDG framework to predict damage and distress from traffic and climate factors. Research indicates that loading speed must be fully considered when evaluating discrepancies between ISLAB2000 predictions and measured structural responses. [2]

Structural Modeling and Element Types

ISLAB2000 utilizes two-dimensional (2-D) plate elements for its slab representation, which requires only modest computer resources and offers a user-friendly interface compared to general-purpose 3-D FE software [3]. Because the program’s structural model is based on thin plate elements that differ from the solid elements used in EverFE 2.24, it results in different nodes and degrees of freedom for each mesh [3].

Subgrade Foundation Models

ISLAB2000 supports multiple subgrade foundation models, including Winkler, elastic solid, Pasternak, Kerr-Vlasov, and Zhemochkin-Sinitsyn-Shtaerman, allowing for varied representations of soil-structure interaction. [4]

Slab-Base Interaction Approaches

The software allows for the modeling of slab-base interaction using three distinct approaches: bonded, unbonded, and Totsky models. [4]

Capabilities for Spatially Varying Properties

ISLAB2000 can model pavement systems with spatially varying properties, enabling the simulation of sections where specific parts of the system differ from others [4]. The software is capable of analyzing partially bonded layers, nonlinear temperature distribution throughout constructed layers, mismatched joints and cracks, and the effect of voids under the slab [1], while allowing users to define an “unlimited” number of nodes, pavement layers, and wheel loads [1].

Geometry and loading characteristics of pavement sections analyzed [1]

Modeling non-uniform subgrade support with ISLAB2000 reveals that spatial variability in foundation stiffness increases localized deflections and causes stress concentrations, leading to premature failures such as fatigue cracking and faulting [5]. Conversely, assuming a uniform subgrade reduces maximum principal stresses and deflections, increasing predicted fatigue life by approximately 1.7 times [5].

Current Limitations and Development Needs

Current implementations of ISLAB2000 require further development to fully consider both loading and thermal gradients simultaneously in layered systems containing discontinuities such as joints and cracks [2]. This capability is identified as a necessary improvement for more accurately calculating stresses across all concrete pavement types and rehabilitation situations [2]. Additionally, ISLAB2000 cannot directly simulate slab deformation caused by moisture variations or permanent deformation at zero temperature and moisture differences, which are apparent during early-age pavement behavior [3].

Applications in EFD Phase II Studies

ISLAB2000 is utilized as the underlying finite element model within the Mechanistic Empirical Pavement Design Guide (MEPDG) to calculate critical stresses and deflections for both new JPCP/CRCP construction and rehabilitation overlays. Despite its state-of-the-art status, discrepancies between ISLAB2000-calculated strains/deflections and measured field values have been documented in benchmark studies such as the AASHO road test and MnROAD. [2]

ISLAB2000 was used alongside EverFE 2.24 in the EFD Phase II study to model slab curvature under varying temperature gradients at the Burlington and Marshalltown field sites. Both models confirmed a permanent effective temperature difference (ΔT_eff) of -8°F to -12°F, validating the field LVDT measurements. [3]

To account for environmental effects not captured by direct simulation, researchers back-estimate equivalent temperature differences from ISLAB2000 simulations to match measured slab curvature profiles. [3]

Comparison of slab curvature behavior between measured and FE-simulated level C profiles path 1 (diagonal direction) – July 17 afternoon [3]

The intercept of the linear regression equation between ISLAB2000’s calculated and actual pavement behavior is related to the permanent component of equivalent temperature difference, including unrecoverable shrinkage and creep. [3]

Burlington site—pavement temperature difference between the top and the bottom of slab with time [3]

Longitudinal Cracking Analysis

In studies of longitudinal cracking in widened JPCP, ISLAB2005—a version evolved from ISLAB2000—utilized a fine mesh discretization with a nominal element size of 6 inches to capture detailed stress distributions [4]. When analyzing longitudinal cracking potential, ISLAB2000-based simulations identified that for standard 12 ft wide slabs, the most critical loading case occurs when loads are positioned 36 inches from the slab edge [4].

Failure mechanism for longitudinal cracking from field investigation [4]

Use in ANN Model Development

The program generated 51,714 solution databases for slab-on-grade JPCP systems using a six-slab assembly (20 ft × 20 ft slabs) with a Winkler (dense liquid) foundation. A standard mesh of 10,004 elements with 10,209 nodes was used. ANN models trained with ISLAB2000 solutions predicted EPCC, kS, RRS, and σPCC with AAE less than 0.4%. [1]

ISLAB2000 finite element model mesh for the six-slab JPCP assembly [1]

Related concepts

  • Backcalculation — ISLAB2000 provides forward solutions for ANN training
  • Artificial Neural Networks — trained with ISLAB2000 solution databases
  • DIPLOMAT — alternative program compared with ISLAB2000 for rigid pavement analysis
  • Pavement Analysis Software

The software is a proprietary revision of ILSL2 and has been used alongside other specialized pavement analysis programs like J-SLAB, KenPAVE, and FEACONS for analyzing systems with various slab sizes, joint conditions, and multiple layers. [6]
