Polypropylene Microfibers

Part of: Fiber-Reinforced Concrete is-component-of
Polypropylene Microfibers is a synthetic microfiber component of Fiber-Reinforced Concrete.
concretefiber-reinforcementmicrofibersynthetic
Also known as: PP microfibers
Created 2026-07-22 · Updated 2026-08-11

Polypropylene microfibers are synthetic components used in fiber-reinforced concrete to mitigate plastic shrinkage cracking during early curing [1]. These fibers are typically incorporated into concrete mixtures at dosages ranging from 0.1% to 0.3% by volume, a concentration shown to be effective in limiting crack growth and reducing shrinkage-induced strains [1]. While higher dosages such as 0.25%, 0.50%, and 1.0% have been investigated for their impact on tensile stresses and strain development, the lower range is particularly noted for its ability to control early-age cracking without significantly altering compressive strength properties due to the fibers’ low elastic modulus [1].

TR-767 Stage 2: Key Findings

The addition of PP microfibers did not significantly affect drying-induced strain rate or final magnitude in restrained ring tests. However, tensile strength increased with PP microfiber percentage for doses up to 1.0% by volume, with the largest relative increase occurring at 0.25%, though efficiency decreased at 1.0% and higher. Furthermore, cracking potential, chloride ion penetration, and 28-day compressive strength all decreased or increased, respectively, with increasing PP microfiber proportion up to 1.0%.

At a macrofiber dosage of 0.5%, alkali-resistant glass fiber-reinforced concrete exhibits a well-formed residual flexural strength extending beyond 1/150 of the span length, a performance not matched by polypropylene or polyvinyl alcohol fibers at equivalent dosages [1].

Flexural strengths of hybrid FRC with PP, AR glass, and PVA macrofibers [1]

Fresh State Properties and Rheology

In self-consolidating fiber-reinforced concrete (SCCF), the addition of polypropylene fibers increases green strength beyond that of high-cement-content mixtures while simultaneously improving flowability, a combination not typically achieved by other additives like nano-clays which reduce flow [2]. When used in self-flowing self-consolidating concrete (SFSCC), polypropylene microfibers reduce overall flowability but enhance shape stability, serving as a functional alternative to nano-clays which also modify these fresh-state properties [2].

Effect of different additives on green strength and flowability for the SCCF mixtures [2]

Workability and Placement Considerations

In pervious concrete, polypropylene microfibers are utilized as workability modifiers to improve placement characteristics without relying solely on chemical admixtures [3]. Regarding macrofibers, polypropylene macrofibers reduce concrete workability more significantly than alkali-resistant (AR) glass or polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) macrofibers due to their inherent water absorption properties [1].

Physical Fiber Specifications

Specific fibrillated polypropylene fiber variants investigated for pervious concrete include a longer 50 mm (2 in.) length and a shorter graded-length variant ranging from 12 mm to 25 mm [3]. These fibrillated polypropylene fibers used in pervious concrete applications typically possess a specific gravity of 0.91 and are incorporated at dosages such as 0.9 kg/m³ (1.5 pcy) or approximately 0.1% by volume [3]. When combined with cellulose microfibers, polypropylene fibers are applied at equal dosages to enhance the freeze-thaw durability and permeability characteristics of pervious concrete overlays [3].

In pervious concrete mixes utilizing single-sized coarse aggregate and a water-to-binder ratio of 0.33, adding polypropylene fiber at 1.5 lb/yd³ increases both the compressive strength and the void content compared to unfibered controls [4]. Furthermore, the addition of polypropylene microfibers in pervious concrete serves to enhance pore connectivity and hydraulic conductivity, which are critical factors for permeability alongside total porosity [4]. While polypropylene (PP) macrofibers reduce compressive strength, alkali-resistant (AR) glass macrofibers provide compressive strength comparable to control samples, making them a superior choice for hybrid fiber-reinforced concrete when combined with PP microfibers [1].

Compressive strength of hybrid FRC with PVA macrofibers [1]

Practical Limitations and Dosage Constraints

PP microfibers increase water demand of concrete, which is a restrictive operational parameter, and the maximum practical dosage recommended is 0.125% (approximately 2 lb/yd³). Beyond 1.0% volume, efficiency of all measured properties significantly decreased. Because microfibers alone cannot provide post-peak strength—requiring macrofibers for post-crack behavior—different materials necessitate varying amounts to achieve desired results; specifically, Polypropylene and polyvinyl alcohol macrofibers require higher dosages (0.25% and 0.1875%, respectively) to provide post-peak flexural strength and toughness, whereas alkali-resistant glass fibers achieve this at a lower dosage of 0.125% [1].

Splitting tensile strengths of hybrid FRC with PP, AR glass, and PVA macrofibers [1]

Standard specifications for ultrathin concrete overlays on brick streets prescribe a dosage rate of 2.5 lb/yd³ of graded fibrillated polypropylene fibers to ensure uniform distribution without clumping [5].

Recommended Mix Proportions

0.125% PP microfiber (2 lb/yd³) combined with 0.25% AR glass macrofiber.

Field Application Parameters

In field evaluations of ultra-thin whitetopping, polypropylene fibrillated fibers were applied at a dosage rate of three pounds per cubic yard of concrete. This specific application method involved blowing the synthetic fibers directly into the mixing drum using a dedicated hopper system. [6]

Fibers being blown into the concrete mixing drum [6]

In Iowa DOT C-4WR mix applications for ultra-thin whitetopping, polypropylene fibers are added at a dosage of one and a half pounds per cubic yard to the northbound lane while leaving the southbound lane as an unfibered control. This specific application utilizes a Class 3 aggregate with a target slump of one inch and employs a two-by-four-inch wood form along the centerline to establish a depth of approximately four inches. [5]

Southbound lane paving completed [5]

Contractor specifications for composite pavement overlays require the incorporation of graded fibrillated polypropylene fibers into C-4 Portland cement concrete at a dosage rate of 2.5 pounds per cubic yard to ensure uniform distribution without clumping. This material is applied as a minimum three-inch overlay on existing surfaces using slip form equipment, forms/vibrating screed, or bridge-deck finishers. [5]

Southbound lane overlay paving [5]

Durability and Chloride Resistance

The inclusion of polypropylene (PP) microfibers at dosages between 0.25% and 1.0% reduces the mean chloride penetration depth by up to 19.1%, thereby enhancing the concrete’s resistance to chloride ion migration [1].

Rapid chloride migration test results for (a) mean chloride penetration depth and (b) migration coefficient [1]

In ultra-thin whitetopping applications, polypropylene microfibers are evaluated alongside other fiber types such as monofilament and proprietary structural fibers to assess interface bonding conditions between the overlay and base layers. [7]

Field studies on polypropylene microfiber-reinforced whitetopping have examined variables including joint spacing configurations (e.g., 4.5 x 4.5, 6 x 6, and 9 x 9-foot sections) and joint preparation methods such as bridging with concrete or stapling #4 rebars to the asphalt surface. [7]
