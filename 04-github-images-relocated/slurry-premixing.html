Slurry Premixing

Part of: Two-Stage Mixing
conceptconcretemixingtwo-stage
Also known as: cement slurry preparation, paste premixing, slurry pre-mixing
Created 2026-05-20 00:00:00+00:00 · Updated 2026-07-30 00:04:50+00:00

The first stage of Two-Stage Mixing, in which cementitious materials (portland cement, fly ash, slag) are combined with water and sometimes chemical admixtures to form a homogeneous paste or slurry before being added to aggregate. Slurry premixing can be performed using High Shear Mixing or normal mixing methods, with high shear providing superior deflocculation and hydration. [1]

Mixing Equipment and Field Systems

The Hydromix (Hydromix Inc.) utilizes continuous high-pressure water jet mixing within an enclosed screw auger to produce slurries in 45-90 seconds [2]. Similarly, the Rotating finger mixer (Concrete Mobility) employs batch mixing via stationary and rotating rods in a horizontal chamber, achieving a mixing time of 30-60 seconds [2]. Compared to drum-type mixers, a horizontal twin shaft mixer requires less mixing time and performs better for high-performance concrete, while offering greater operational economy and throughput [2].

Specific Mixing Energy and Viscosity Optimization

Optimizing specific mixing energy (SME) to approximately 5.5 kJ/kg enhances cement slurry properties by promoting greater deflocculation of cement particles, which increases the total surface area of exposed cement and improves water-cement contact [1]. Furthermore, measuring plastic viscosity allows for the determination of the minimum mixing energy required to achieve slurry deflocculation, beyond which further increases in mixing energy do not improve slurry properties [1].

Two-Step and SEC Mixing Techniques

The sand-enveloped-with-cement (SEC) technique involves adding water at two separate times and can achieve up to 25% higher compressive strengths with significantly decreased bleeding capacity [2]. This two-step mixing method can increase 28-day compressive strength by approximately 8% to 17% compared to normal mixing methods, while also reducing the total amount of water and cement required in the mixture [2]. By coating aggregate particles with a low water-to-cement ratio slurry prior to adding the remaining mixing water, the final water-to-cement ratio at the paste/aggregate interface is reduced and the overall thickness of the interfacial transition zone layer decreases [1]. Consequently, adding water in two stages creates a greater gel-to-space ratio in the interfacial transition zone (ITZ), resulting in lower porosity, increased micro-hardness, and a more intimate bond between aggregate and paste [1].

Micro-hardness at age of 28 days and 0.45 W–C ratio (from Tamimi 1994) [2]

Alternatively, the SEC technique involves adding water in three distinct stages—initially with aggregate for 30 seconds, then with cement for 60 seconds, and finally the remainder for 90 seconds—to reduce bleeding and improve compressive strength [1].

Effects of mixing method on the slump of fresh concrete [1]

Admixture Application Strategies

In high-performance concrete applications, adding superplasticizers in water as a single dose with sufficient mixing time enhances their function more effectively than other addition methods [2]. Delaying the addition of high-range water-reducing admixtures reduces their absorption by cement paste, thereby lowering the amount of water needed to maintain constant fluidity [2]. Regarding application methods, a water reducer can be added in the slurry or sprayed on aggregate; however, it is recommended to spray AEA onto aggregate during second stage instead of adding to slurry.

Impact of Admixtures on Slurry Properties

  • Air-entraining agent (AEA): adding AEA in the slurry reduces air entrainment effectiveness — fine cementitious particles absorb AEA
