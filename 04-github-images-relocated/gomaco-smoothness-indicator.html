GOMACO Smoothness Indicator (GSI)

Part of: Pavement Smoothness
conceptconstructionequipmentprofilingreal-time-monitoring
Also known as: GSI
Created 2026-05-20 00:00:00+00:00 · Updated 2026-07-30 00:04:50+00:00

A real-time pavement profile measurement system developed by GOMACO Corporation for use on plastic concrete during slip-form paving operations. The GSI uses multiple wheel-path sensors mounted to the paver and/or a trailing vehicle to measure surface profile immediately behind the paving operation. [1]

Field Evaluation Overview

The GSI was evaluated on U.S. Highway 30 near Le Grand, Iowa over six days of paving (July 2004). Three sensor arrangements were tested: (1) four traces behind the paver pan plus two after the cure/texture machine, (2) three traces behind the pan plus one after the V-screed plus two before the texture/cure machine, and (3) four traces on a vehicle after the blade and broom operation. [1]

GSI arrangement over each wheel path (Arrangement 1) [1]

System Configuration and Hardware

The GSI system utilizes a total of six wheel path sensors and two computers, with one computer mounted to the slip-form paver and the second mounted to a dedicated trailing vehicle. This configuration allows for simultaneous data collection across multiple points in the paving train. [1]

GSI testing on U.S. Hwy 30 westbound from Tama to Le Grand, Iowa [1]

Coordinate control for these scanning systems is achieved through tracking total stations that monitor prisms mounted on the paver, combined with paver-mounted tilt sensors to calculate three-dimensional coordinates [2], while data processing involves clustering algorithms that remove background noise and string lines by identifying points within specific Euclidean distance thresholds [2].

Anticipated vision of laser scanning concrete thickness profiler [2]

Profiling Methodology

The GSI profiler was utilized to continuously measure pavement profiles along the entire length of a project to evaluate stringless technology performance on a new slipform paver. [3]

Gomaco GSI profiler [3]

Detection Capabilities and Findings

The system detected Dowel Basket Ripple with enough clarity to warn the paving crew, though it was found to include extraneous content in the wavelength range near three feet, which was subsequently corrected via software change. Furthermore, the system is capable of identifying the impact of stringless total station spacing on smoothness and verifying the effectiveness of subsequent changes to the paving process [4].

PSD Analysis Showing Joint Spacing at 15’ and Concrete Loads at 13’ with Associated Harmonic Wavelengths Contributing to Pavement Roughness [4]

Surface roughness in concrete pavements is significantly influenced by the dowel bar basket ripple, which exhibits larger ripple amplitudes in outside wheel tracks than in inside wheel tracks; however, applying an auto-float after the float pan can significantly reduce this specific source of surface undulation [1].

Simulated profilograph trace, passing lane outside trace [1]

Measurement Accuracy and Limitations

The system is not able to produce the same absolute IRI on plastic concrete as inertial profilers on hardened concrete [1]. Consequently, real-time profiles generated by the system are typically approximately 100 in/mi, which is higher than hardened profile measurements that average around 65 in/mi [4].

RTP surface roughness plots for nine 100-meter sections [1]

Laser Scanning Technology

Laser scanning systems mounted on slipform pavers can determine pavement thickness in real time by comparing pre-paving subgrade scans to post-paving surface profiles, eliminating the need for destructive core sampling [2].

Performing laser scan on the subbase level [2]

Alternative Measurement Methods

Although ultrasonic and eddy current sensors offer a nondestructive alternative for measuring thickness at discrete points, they lack the continuous coverage provided by laser scanning [2]; however, Ultrasonic pulse velocity (UPV) measurements through concrete can determine the set time of the material, with data indicating that set time occurred between 5 and 8 hours in monitored sections [5].

Eddy current sensor for concrete thickness measurement [2]

The device has an operating range distance from the pavement surface that aids in eliminating wind effects on the output. [1]

The system can be mounted on any portion of the paving train, including slip-form pavers, mechanical floats, cure carts, or bridges. [1]

Sensors mounted to texture and curing machine Figure 9. Train configuration for the sensors mounted on the paving machine and on the curing and tining machines [1]

Shrouds should be placed around the sensors when employed in the slip-form paving train to prevent concrete and curing material splatter from damaging the sensors or adversely impacting the profile measurements. [1]

RTP beams attached in each wheel track [1]

The GSI can be operated in conjunction with all brands of equipment employed in the paving train. [1]

Each device can be referenced to longitudinal stationing for referencing surface imperfections for modification of the profile in the plastic or hardened concrete state. [1]

Reduction in roughness by hand finishing [1]

The Ames Engineering Vehicle arrangement and the Gomaco smoothness indicator (GSI) device offered potential for future mapping of pavement surfaces specifically for overlay designs. [3]
