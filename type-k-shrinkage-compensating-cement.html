Type K Shrinkage-Compensating Cement

Part of: Cementitious Materials & Chemistry is-expansive-hydraulic-cement-type-of
Type K Shrinkage-Compensating Cement is an expansive hydraulic cement type within Cementitious Materials & Chemistry.
cementconcreteexpansiveshrinkage-compensation
Also known as: Type K SCC, shrinkage-compensating cement
Created 2026-07-22 · Updated 2026-08-20

Type K shrinkage-compensating cement is an expansive hydraulic cement composed of calcium sulfoaluminate (ye’elimite), anhydrite, and lime. Its hydration generates ettringite through mechanisms involving both crystal growth and surface water adsorption swelling [1]. This controlled internal expansion counteracts the plastic and drying shrinkage of concrete, thereby reducing tensile strain and limiting crack widths [1].

Key findings

  • Found that adding Type K shrinkage-compensating cement to the binder significantly reduces early-age plastic-shrinkage manifestations and crack width [1].

Type K Cement Shrinkage Mitigation

Type K shrinkage-compensating cement is an expansive hydraulic cement that utilizes calcium sulfoaluminate compounds to generate controlled expansion during early hydration [1]. This expansion primarily results from the formation of ettringite, which counteracts concrete shrinkage through mechanisms involving crystal growth and surface water adsorption [1]. By offsetting plastic and drying shrinkage, this internal expansion reduces tensile strain and limits crack widths in the resulting concrete structure [1].

Final tensile strain chart for Specimen 0K. [1]

The figure displays a plot of final tensile strain measured across the length of a concrete slab specimen labeled as ‘Specimen 0K’.

Research problem — The use of Type K shrinkage-compensating cement creates a trade-off where increased proportions narrow crack widths but simultaneously accelerate capillary-pressure buildup and reduce resistance to plastic shrinkage, thereby endangering concrete durability [1]. The finer particle size and water consumption required for ettringite formation compromise workability and bleeding, which hinders proper placement and increases the vulnerability of bridge-deck slabs to early-age cracking [1].

Onset and rate of plastic shrinkage cracking for Specimens 0-15FA, 7.5K-15FA, and 15K-15FA [1]

The figure plots crack width against time after concrete placement to compare the performance of three different mixtures. State of the art — Type K shrinkage-compensating cement is classified under ASTM C845 and functions by generating expansive ettringite to counteract drying and plastic shrinkage [1]. The expansive effect is often amplified when combined with supplementary cementitious materials like fly ash or silica fume, provided that high-range water-reducing admixtures are used to manage the increased water demand and reduced workability [1].

Findings from the reports

  • Added Type K shrinkage-compensating cement consistently accelerated early-age capillary pressure development across all binder mixes, regardless of the supplementary cementitious material used [1].
  • Higher dosages of Type K cement reduced plastic-shrinkage crack widths measured six hours after casting relative to plain concrete [1].
  • Digital image correlation confirmed a marked decline in peak tensile strain at potential cracking sites as the dosage of Type K cement increased [1].
  • In mixes containing fly ash or silica fume, the expansive cement either eliminated visible cracking or slowed crack propagation when workability was maintained with a superplasticizer [1].
  • The reduction in early-age plastic-shrinkage manifestations is attributed to the expansive action of Type K cement, which originates from water adsorption onto colloidal ettringite particles rather than crystal growth [1].
  • Every increase in Type K content accelerated the development of capillary pressure, a phenomenon that outweighs the crack-width advantage and degrades overall shrinkage resistance [1].
  • The authors advised against incorporating Type K cement in final bridge-deck mix designs because the accelerated capillary pressure development negates its shrinkage-mitigating benefits [1].

Novelty & rigor — This work introduces a novel experimental framework that simultaneously tracks early-age capillary pressure and surface tensile strain to quantify the crack-mitigation mechanisms of Type K cement in restrained bridge-deck concrete [1]. The methodology is distinct for its systematic modification of standard plastic-shrinkage testing protocols, specifically by substituting fiber reinforcement with varying dosages of expansive cement and employing high-resolution digital image correlation to isolate the material’s expansive action [1]. Reproducibility is ensured through a detailed specification of chemical limits for the Type K cement, precise mixing sequences, and standardized environmental chamber conditions that allow for faithful replication of the sensor-driven test protocol [1].

This behavior aligns with literature stating that Type K cement increases the rate of capillary pressure development, a phenomenon that becomes more evident as the content of this expansive hydraulic cement increases.

Implications — Designers should exclude Type K cement from bridge-deck mix designs because its accelerated capillary pressure buildup and durability risks outweigh early-age crack control benefits [1]. Specifiers ought to consider alternative supplementary binders, such as Class F fly ash, to avoid the workability issues and volume change concerns associated with Type K cement [1].

Limitations — The material can accelerate capillary pressure development, which undermines its intended benefit of reducing plastic shrinkage [1]. Fresh concrete workability and bleeding are reduced, often requiring additional admixtures to maintain desired consistency [1]. The optimal dosage for minimizing tensile strain remains uncertain due to measurement inconsistencies and the need for further testing [1].
